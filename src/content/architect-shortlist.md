# Austin-Area MCM Architect Shortlist

Prepared for George Waked & Taylor — custom mid-century-modern home, Austin metro / Hill Country, $1M–$2M all-in, 12–24 month move-in target, heavy client co-design.

Research date: June 2026. Sources: firm websites, Texas Architect / Texas Society of Architects, Dwell, Dezeen, Architectural Record, Architect Magazine, Residential Design, AIA Austin (incl. Homes Tour 2021–2025), Houzz, and project press. Every dollar figure and availability signal that could not be confirmed from a primary source is marked **(unverified)** or **(estimate)**. MCM-fit star ratings are analytical judgments, not the firms' self-descriptions.

---

## TL;DR — Top 5 to interview first

1. **Nick Deaver / Jes Deaver Architecture** — the purest MCM + co-design match in Austin (literally restores Fehr & Granger glass houses). Caveat: 2-person studio, expect a waitlist.
2. **Matt Fajkus Architecture** — repeatedly works in an explicit MCM idiom, brand-built on a client-and-site-driven (not auteur) process, budget-aligned, boutique.
3. **Webber + Studio** — the only Austin firm that explicitly markets a midcentury-modern practice, with a "the best designer for your home is you" co-design ethos; budget squarely in range.
4. **A Parallel Architecture** — the best-documented collaborative philosophy of any firm here ("parallel objectives, common goal"), crisp contemporary-MCM, named proof of Texas-heat and site-driven design.
5. **Pollen Architecture & Design** — reconceived a 1957 Case-Study-era house as their own home; genuine mid-century soul, strong site/climate instincts, principal-level access. Caveat: smallest built track record at full ground-up scale.

**Standout finding:** Austin has very few *true* period-MCM specialists — most elite local firms do warm "regional/contemporary modernism" and would be steered *toward* MCM rather than working *from* it. The handful that genuinely live in the mid-century idiom (Deaver, Webber, Fajkus, Pollen) are mostly small boutiques, so the binding constraint for a 12–24 month target is **queue/availability, not capability** — engage your top choices early.

**Firms profiled:** 18 in depth + 3 ruled out (Dick Clark + Associates, Heyl Homes, "KEM"). KEM Studio/KEM Architects is a **Kansas City** firm — there is no Austin firm by that name; dropped (see Skip list).

## Nick Deaver / Jes Deaver Architecture

**Principals:** Nick Deaver, AIA and daughter Jes Deaver, AIA. Texas Tech-trained; Nick previously practiced at Moore Grover Harper (Connecticut), absorbing Charles Moore's "sense of place" ethos.
**Location / size:** 606 Highland Ave, Austin, TX 78703. Very small studio — 2 registered architects, ~2 staff. Maximum principal attention; minimum capacity.

**Aesthetic signature:** Quiet, place-rooted regional modernism. Natural light, generous spatial flow, and a deliberate dissolving of the line between interior and exterior. Restrained palettes — wood, glass, limestone — with a reverence for site and heritage trees and an explicit goal of minimal environmental footprint. They are the clearest mid-century-literate practice in Austin: they restore actual MCM landmarks rather than referencing the style.

**MCM fit: ★★★★★.** RaveOn is a celebrated restoration of a **1956 Fehr & Granger glass house** — they work fluently inside authentic mid-century language while solving for modern livability. Exactly an MCM couple's profile.

**Site-responsive strength:** Exceptional and explicit. RaveOn preserved the house's hover over a steep site under a live-oak "cathedral." OFFbeat wrapped a new addition around two heritage live oaks on a rhombus-shaped infill lot. Site/tree response is a defining method, not an afterthought.

**Texas-heat competence:** Strong indirect evidence — indoor-outdoor dissolving, deep integration with mature oak shade, "minimal demands on the environment." No published energy-modeling specifics **(unverified)**.

**Collaborative process:** Collaborator, deeply. Self-described "father and daughter design collaboration that emphasizes dialogue, discovery," producing "architecture reflective of each client," with particular attention to the owner–builder–artisan relationship. Ideal for co-design.

**Typical budget range:** Not published **(unverified)**. Award-level custom and high-craft renovation; a $1–2M build is plausible, but as a 2-person studio they take very few projects — confirm capacity and fee structure early.

**Notable residential projects:**
- **RaveOn** — Austin (Northwest Hills), 2018. Restoration/reimagining of a 1956 Fehr & Granger MCM glass house on a steep oak-covered site; 2022 Residential Design Award. https://www.archdaily.com/967565/raveon-nick-deaver-architect
- **OFFbeat** — Austin (Travis Heights), completed Nov 2023. 2,128 sf; 1930s cottage reimagined modern around two heritage oaks; 2024 AIA Austin Design Award of Excellence + Preservation Award. https://ndjda.com/offbeat
- **Three Stones** — Austin. Modern residence. https://archello.com/project/three-stones

**Recent activity / availability:** Highly active and decorated (multiple 2022 and 2024 awards). As a 2-person studio, **availability is the principal risk** — engage early; expect a waitlist.

**Why they fit / why not:** *Fit* — likely the single best stylistic and process match in the metro: literal MCM restorer, deeply collaborative, site/tree-driven. *Not* — tiny, selective studio means slower pace and a bespoke (not turnkey) experience; capacity is the open question for a 12–24 month target.

**Website / contact:** https://ndjda.com — 512-494-9808. AIA profile: https://aiaaustin.org/architects/ndjda/
**Pull-quote:** "Buildings can awaken the senses, reconnecting us with nature… celebrating modesty, beauty and meaning." — https://aiaaustin.org/architects/ndjda/

## Matt Fajkus Architecture (MF Architecture)

**Principal:** Matt Fajkus, AIA, LEED AP (founded 2010; M.Arch Harvard GSD; faculty at UT Austin School of Architecture). Senior associate Sarah Johnson.
**Location / size:** 900 East 6th St, Suite 100, Austin, TX 78702. Small studio (~8 team members). Boutique, principal-accessible.

**Aesthetic signature:** The most explicitly MCM-fluent of the larger-profile firms. Honest natural materials (Douglas Fir siding, breeze/screen blocks), low "humble" rooflines, strong solid-vs-glass façade contrasts, open plans, clerestories and courtyards for cross-ventilation, and a strong indoor-outdoor connection. Several projects (Split House, Re-Open House, Alegria) are explicit MCM reinterpretations or MCM renovations. Clean, restrained, sustainability-driven.

**MCM fit: ★★★★½.** Repeatedly and deliberately works in the MCM idiom — Split House "incorporates elements of the Mid-Century Modern typology… through massing, scale, and materiality"; Re-Open House was remodeled "in the spirit of the original Mid-Century Modern house"; Alegria is a mid-century renovation.

**Site-responsive strength:** Philosophy is site-first. Inverse House inverts public/private to suit the lot; Split House threads a constrained urban lot between two oversized neighbors via a central spine and courtyards; Slim House is a narrow infill addition. Strongest on tight urban lots; large-scale Hill Country ground-up examples are less documented **(unverified)** — pressure-test if your site is rural/dramatic.

**Texas-heat competence:** Strong. Explicit passive-design practice — energy efficiency, passive daylighting, cross-ventilation via courtyards/clerestories/sliding glass, healthy-material selection. 2019 AIA National Design Award (Westlake Dermatology Marble Falls).

**Collaborative process:** Collaborator, not auteur — and it's their explicit brand: "At the heart of the firm's process is collaboration — between architect and client, between design and environment." Design is "driven by the client, the site, and the functional requirements, rather than a singular, preconceived aesthetic." Best-documented co-design posture among the mid-profile firms. (A widely-cited "930 reviews / 4.9" Houzz figure could not be confirmed in text — **unverified**.)

**Typical budget range:** No published fees **(estimate)**. Portfolio spans modest remodels to full custom homes; "welcomes residential projects small and large." A $1–2M all-in custom home fits comfortably.

**Notable residential projects:**
- **Split House** — Zilker, Austin, 2019. MCM reinterpretation on a tight lot; Douglas Fir + breeze blocks. https://www.mfarchitecture.com/work-split-house
- **Re-Open House** — Brentwood, Austin, 2017. Bungalow remodel "in the spirit of the original Mid-Century Modern house." https://www.mfarchitecture.com/work-reopen-house
- **Inverse House** — Austin. Public spaces up, private down — a site-driven inversion. https://www.mfarchitecture.com/work-inverse-house
- **Slim House** — Tarrytown, Austin. Modern addition to a 1933 house. https://www.mfarchitecture.com/work-slim-house
- **Alegria** — Austin. Mid-century home renovation.

**Recent activity / availability:** Very active and decorated — multiple 2024 SARA national awards, 2023 AIA Austin Design Award, AIA Homes Tour appearances. Academic + practice load on the principal could lengthen timelines **(availability unverified)**.

**Why they fit / why not:** *Fit* — strongest MCM vocabulary plus an explicitly client-and-site-driven process; boutique scale. *Not* — most published work is urban-infill / remodel scale; confirm comparable large ground-up Hill Country examples if that's your site.

**Website / contact:** https://www.mfarchitecture.com — (512) 432-5137 — info@mfarchitecture.com
**Pull-quote:** "The design work of Matt Fajkus Architecture is based on the belief that each project is unique and it should be driven by the client, the site, and the functional requirements, rather than a singular, preconceived aesthetic." — https://www.mfarchitecture.com/practice

## Webber + Studio, Architects

**Principals:** David Webber, AIA (founder/CEO, B.Arch UT Austin 1992; firm founded 1997); Thomas Tornbjerg (Managing Principal, Residential — previously project architect at Bercy Chen Studio on Cascading Creek House and Peninsula Residence); Stefan Bader, AIA; Marek Hnizda, AIA LEED AP.
**Location / size:** 1220 Lavaca St., Austin 78701. Mid-size boutique — a named, lean leadership team, residential-focused. 25+ years across TX, NM, CO.

**Aesthetic signature:** Crisp, restrained Austin modernism governed by four stated principles — Functionalism, Expressionism, Regionalism, Minimalism. Clean volumes, floor-to-ceiling glass, warm natural materials (redwood, brick, copper accents), and careful siting for views/breezes/trees. Their remodel work shows genuine MCM literacy — e.g., a 1960s Austin house rebuilt with pale redwood flooring, neutral walls, buffed-copper accents, and handmade brick. Detailing aims for "effortless" inevitability over statement-making.

**MCM fit: ★★★★½.** The strongest *explicit* MCM signal in Austin — David Webber gave an Austin Monthly interview specifically on "Classic Home Styles in Austin: Midcentury Modern," and the firm cites one of its "most awarded Midcentury Modern remodels." They both restore period MCM homes and design new modernism in the idiom.

**Site-responsive strength:** Good and client-evidenced. Testimonials cite the firm positioning homes to "take full advantage of views, breezes, trees and other special site characteristics." Tarrytown remodel: full-height glass back wall, no-threshold accessible main floor. Dripping Springs residence won a Rethinking the Future Private Residence Award. AIA Austin Homes Tour homes: Fairfield House, Shoal Creek, Mount Bonnell, Brady Lane.

**Texas-heat competence:** Good (regionalism is an explicit pillar; documented use of glass walls sited for breezes/shade trees). Less COTE-style hard-sustainability documentation than Lake|Flato **(specific passive-cooling detailing partly unverified)**.

**Collaborative process:** Explicitly client-co-design — the best cultural fit on this list. Stated: "The best designer for your perfect home is you. We strongly believe that everyone can contribute to the design process." And: "Your bespoke home is the creative collaboration between your distinctive tastes and our decades of design experience." Literally framed as co-authorship.

**Typical budget range:** Markets "luxury homes"; a $1–2M custom build is squarely in their wheelhouse **(published fee range unverified)**. Data point: a 6,900 sf Webber-rebuilt MCM home listed at $7.595M (a sale price including land/market premium — not a build cost).

**Notable residential projects:**
- **Mid-Century Modern Remodel + Addition** — Austin. 1960s house rebuilt; redwood, brick, copper. https://www.houzz.com/photos/mid-century-modern-remodel-addition-contemporary-staircase-austin-phvw-vp~53167459
- **Dripping Springs Residence** — Rethinking the Future award winner.
- **Tarrytown Remodel + Addition** — Austin. Accessible main floor, glass back wall, "secret garden."
- AIA Homes Tour homes: Fairfield House, Shoal Creek, Mount Bonnell, Brady Lane.

**Recent activity / availability:** Steady, repeatedly press-featured, multiple recent AIA Homes Tour entries. Boutique scale suggests more direct principal access than the large firms **(current queue unverified)**.

**Why they fit / why not:** *Fit* — explicitly MCM-fluent, explicitly co-design in philosophy, boutique principal attention, budget-aligned, deep Austin knowledge. The single best on-paper match for an MCM co-design couple. *Not* — lighter heat-engineering credentialing than Lake|Flato; smaller bench depth if the project gets complex; their new-build modernism can read "contemporary clean" rather than strictly period MCM.

**Website / contact:** https://webberstudio.com — 512-236-1032
**Pull-quote:** "The best designer for your perfect home is you. We strongly believe that everyone can contribute to the design process." — https://webberstudio.com/residential-design-in-austin-tx/

## A Parallel Architecture

**Principals:** Eric Barth, AIA (UT Austin valedictorian) and Ryan Burke, AIA (UT Austin highest honors, 2002). Founded 2008; deliberately did not put surnames "above the door."
**Location / size:** 1204 E Cesar Chavez St, Suite A, Austin 78702. Boutique — ~9 staff (2 principals + designers/associates). High principal involvement.

**Aesthetic signature:** Self-described "crafted modernism" — refined, restrained, precise. Rigorous proportion and detailing; quiet natural palettes (open-grained cypress ceilings, rift white oak, old-growth walnut, painted brick); courtyard/indoor-outdoor organization; homes "striking in presence yet deeply livable" — "quietly iconic." Crisper and more overtly modern than the earthier regional firms — arguably the closest of the boutiques to a contemporary-MCM sensibility.

**MCM fit: ★★★★.** Strong stylistic adjacency — clarity, livability, courtyard/indoor-outdoor organization, restraint. No evidence of period-MCM restoration (unlike Deaver/Webber), so they'd do a contemporary MCM-inflected new build rather than work from mid-century stock. Confirm enthusiasm for an explicitly mid-century brief vs. their own contemporary idiom.

**Site-responsive strength:** Excellent. Three Courts Residence (4,800 sf over Lake Austin) is woven through existing brick walls and "tucked under sprawling canopies of heritage oaks," organized around three overlapping courtyards. Canopy House (2,902 sf) is a U-shaped footprint weaving through oak/pecan trees, protecting root zones.

**Texas-heat competence:** Demonstrated and specific. Canopy House's floating canopy "shelters the outdoor living spaces from the intense Texas climate and helps channel prevailing breezes," with seasonal shading designed in — the most explicit named-project heat evidence among the boutiques.

**Collaborative process:** Strongest documented collaborative ethos here. The firm name comes from cognitive science's "parallel architecture" (distributed processing); they state expertise "isn't centralized in a single authority but distributed across a network," and explicitly reject the "individual brilliance" auteur model. Verbatim: "We embrace every project as an opportunity to collaborate and create, working in concert with client, consultant and contractor to form an inspired team with parallel objectives and a common goal."

**Typical budget range:** Boutique custom homes of 2,900–4,800 sf in premium locations; a $1.5M home is plausibly mid-range **(estimate; no published fees)**.

**Notable residential projects:**
- **Three Courts Residence** — Lake Austin. 4,800 sf, three-courtyard organization through heritage oaks. https://www.aparallel.com/three-courts-residence
- **Canopy House** — Austin. 2,902 sf walled compound; 2019 American Architecture Award; Dwell-featured. https://www.aparallel.com/canopy-house
- **Terrace Mountain Residence** — Austin. 2026 AIA Austin Design Award Merit + Texas Society of Architects Design Award.
- **Timberline Residence** — 2025 AIA Austin Homes Tour.
- **Rollingwood Residence** — 2024 AIA Austin Design Award Merit.

**Recent activity / availability:** Very active and rising — 2025 Forbes "America's Best-in-State Residential Architects"; 2024 & 2026 AIA Austin merits; 2025 Homes Tour. Strong demand likely means a queue **(unverified)**.

**Why they fit / why not:** *Fit* — most explicitly collaborative process here; crisp contemporary-modern closest to a new-build MCM sensibility; named Texas-heat/site proof; boutique principal access; plausible budget. *Not* — no track record with actual mid-century stock; rising acclaim may lengthen their queue and firm up fees.

**Website / contact:** https://www.aparallel.com — 512.464.1199 — info@aparallel.com

## Pollen Architecture & Design

**Principals:** Elizabeth Alford, AIA (co-founder; native Austinite; M.Arch Columbia; apprenticed at Smith-Miller+Hawkinson and Anderson/Schwartz NYC; taught at UT Austin 2003–2017) with co-founder/artist Michael Young. Founded 2008.
**Location / size:** 1224 E 12th St #210, Austin 78702. Small boutique — ~5–6 people. Principal-led, high client access.

**Aesthetic signature:** Quiet, refined modernism in genuine dialogue with mid-century restraint. The firm's own home, Balcones House, is a ground-up reconception of a **1957 Case-Study-era house**, deliberately preserving its modest scale and limestone rubble walls. Hallmarks: indoor-outdoor connection, careful solar orientation and daylighting, straight-grain pine and plaster, sliding walls that fully retract for cross-ventilation. Restrained and craft-driven, not flashy.

**MCM fit: ★★★★.** The only firm here with a built project rooted explicitly in the Case-Study/mid-century tradition and a stated ethos of mid-century restraint and modest scale. Genuine MCM DNA. Slightly behind Fajkus/Deaver only because the residential portfolio is smaller and the formal language is "quiet modern" rather than overtly MCM-signature.

**Site-responsive strength:** Strong. Balcones House perches on a fractured limestone cliff along the Balcones fault — rooms turned downhill into ravine treetops or uphill into rock gardens; roof geometry twists to respond to the cliff and harvest water.

**Texas-heat competence:** Strong. Passive cooling via fully-retracting sliding windows; continuous clerestory admitting controlled southern light; water-harvesting roof; careful solar-orientation study.

**Collaborative process:** Collaborator. Stated ethos centers on "the collaborative nature of the design process" and listening carefully to clients. Small studio = principal-level co-design. Best of Houzz 2023 & 2024, though no substantive written third-party testimonials are publicly posted **(collaboration depth corroborated by ethos and structure, not testimonials)**.

**Typical budget range:** No published figures **(estimate)**. Boutique high-craft modern in Austin typically runs into the $1M+ all-in range; Balcones House at 2,700 sf is a smaller-footprint, high-craft project — a $1–2M budget is plausibly well-matched.

**Notable residential projects:**
- **Balcones House** — Austin (Balcones fault cliff), 2011, 2,700 sf. Reconception of a 1957 house; firm's own residence; AIA Homes Tour 2012. https://www.archdaily.com/805995/balcones-house-pollen-architecture-and-design
- **Haskell Street House** — Austin **(detail unverified)**.
- **Crestway Remodel**, **Highland Hills Renovation**, **Windsor Park Addition** — Austin residential renovations.

**Recent activity / availability:** Pivoted toward houses since ~2020; returned to the AIA Homes Tour in 2024; Best of Houzz 2023 & 2024. A small studio actively building a residential book — may have more bandwidth/hunger for a marquee MCM commission than the busier larger firms **(availability unverified, but the residential pivot is a positive signal)**.

**Why they fit / why not:** *Fit* — possibly the best cultural fit for an MCM co-design couple (literally rebuilt a 1957 Case-Study house), strong site + climate instincts, small principal-led studio. *Not* — smallest built residential portfolio of the strong fits; thinnest public budget/testimonial data — diligence the question of whether they've delivered a full $1–2M ground-up recently.

**Website / contact:** https://www.pollenarchitecture.com — (512) 499-0888 — info@pollenarchitecture.com
**Pull-quote:** Pollen aims "to connect architecture with the natural environment, and to celebrate materiality and the collaborative nature of the design process." — https://www.pollenarchitecture.com/studio/

## Clayton Korte

**Principals:** Paul Clayton (AIA, LEED AP) and Brian Korte (FAIA); partner Kristen Brown (AIA, RID); Emily Little (FAIA) is Partner Emerita. Traces to 1983 (formerly Clayton & Little).
**Location / size:** Austin and San Antonio. Mid-to-large studio (~25+ named leadership/staff across architecture and interiors) — the most institutional of the boutique-tier firms. Client attention flows through a project team, not necessarily a principal day-to-day.

**Aesthetic signature:** Warm, materially-rich modernism with a strong craft and adaptive-reuse streak. Raw structural materials (shotcrete, concrete, limestone) paired with tactile warm woods (vertical-grain Douglas fir ceilings, white oak, walnut), plaster, terrazzo. Restrained additions that defer to existing structures; lots of embedding buildings into Hill Country topography. Less "glass box," more quiet, earthy, handcrafted. Strong integrated interiors.

**MCM fit: ★★★★½.** The deepest *authentic*-MCM renovation track record in the metro: Balcones Residence (a **1950s Roland Roessner** house), Ridgecrest, and Ridge Oak. They clearly understand MCM vocabulary — cantilevers, glazing-heavy elevations, indoor-outdoor flow — and respect original modernist gestures. Caveat: much headline work is adaptive reuse/hospitality and earthy-rustic rather than crisp MCM new-build — confirm appetite for a from-scratch MCM.

**Site-responsive strength:** Outstanding. Hill Country Wine Cave (Blanco River; 1,405 sf excavated into a limestone hillside) is a landmark of site-driven design. Balcones preserved a cantilevered concrete balcony over a slope; additions are "minimally discernible from the street." Ridgecrest uses floor-to-ceiling glazing to frame West Lake Hills/downtown views.

**Texas-heat competence:** Strong. Ridgecrest concentrates glazing on the southern wall with deep framing; the wine cave exploits subterranean thermal mass; limestone/concrete palette and reuse ethic imply climate-conscious orientation.

**Collaborative process:** **Unverified, leaning team-delivered-vision.** Philosophy is craft- and place-driven; the Windsor Residence narrative notes interiors "reflect the sensibility of the home's owner." But as the largest firm in this tier, expect a structured studio process rather than principal-as-co-designer. Confirm who leads your project.

**Typical budget range:** Upper tier **(estimate)**. Likely most comfortable at $2M+ all-in; a $1–2M MCM is achievable but at the lower bound of their typical work, especially new construction.

**Notable residential projects:**
- **Hill Country Wine Cave** — Blanco River, TX, 2021. 1,405 sf embedded in limestone hillside; 2021 RADA Honor. https://claytonkorte.com/project/hill-country-wine-cave/
- **Balcones Residence** — Austin. Renovation of a 1950s Roland Roessner MCM house; 2,680 sf; 2017 Preservation Austin Merit; 2018 AIA Austin Homes Tour. https://claytonkorte.com/project/balcones-residence/
- **Ridgecrest Residence** — West Lake Hills. Renovation/expansion of a 1970s home to 5,574 sf. https://claytonkorte.com/project/ridgecrest-residence/
- **Ridge Oak Residence** — Austin. Mid-century home transformation.
- **Windsor Residence** — Tarrytown. 2,100 sf expansion of a 1940s brick house; Austin Home 2026 Best Kitchen.

**Recent activity / availability:** Very active and decorated. Busy, in-demand — expect a queue **(unverified)**.

**Why they fit / why not:** *Fit* — deepest authentic-MCM renovation credentials and elite site/craft execution. *Not* — largest firm in this tier (less principal face-time), much fame is adaptive reuse/hospitality, skews to higher budgets; a $1.5M new-build couple wanting intimate principal collaboration may feel like a small fish.

**Website / contact:** https://claytonkorte.com — contact via website form; direct phone/email not published **(unverified)**.
**Pull-quote:** "Clayton Korte creates places that celebrate their environment and exalt the human experience at the intersection of architecture, interiors, authenticity, and craft." — https://claytonkorte.com/about/

## Jay Corder, Architect

**Principal:** Jay Corder, AIA (UT Austin). Small, principal-named boutique studio.
**Location / size:** 2700 W Anderson Ln, Suite 309, Austin 78757. Small boutique; exact headcount **(unverified)**.

**Aesthetic signature:** Warm modernism, not cold minimalism. Open layouts, abundant natural light, strong indoor-outdoor connection, refined and restrained material palettes. Comfortable across new-build modern, "transitional" interiors, and sensitive MCM renovation/restoration. Material selection and contextual scale are explicit drivers ("restraint, the respect of context"). Range spans Wrightian, one-story courtyard minimalism, and lake/ranch vernacular.

**MCM fit: ★★★★.** Has explicitly executed MCM restoration/addition work (Pemberton), and press describes one North Austin project as "decidedly mid-century inspired." Modernism is core, but a meaningful share of the portfolio is transitional/contextual — a strong fit who will likely steer toward a refined contemporary-MCM hybrid.

**Site-responsive strength:** Demonstrated. Pemberton handled a steep rear slope and stripped decades of modifications to recover original modernist lines. Clearview addressed a tight site with a Wrightian solution. Enfield solved a noisy corner lot while maximizing light/outdoor access.

**Texas-heat competence:** Implied through courtyard schemes and orientation for light control (Shoal Creek, Cherry Lane courtyards). No explicit passive-cooling/energy claims found **(unverified)**.

**Collaborative process:** Collaborator, strongly self-described: "We believe in a reciprocal client relationship and want to be listening as much as we are talking," and "It's on me to develop a trustworthy partnership with each of my clients." Good fit for a co-design couple.

**Typical budget range:** Not published **(unverified)**. Portfolio (Horseshoe Bay, Old Enfield, Pemberton) reads upper-middle to high-end custom, consistent with $1–2M.

**Notable residential projects:**
- **Pemberton Residence** — Austin. MCM addition/remodel recovering original modernist features; steep-slope site. https://www.jaycorder.com/projects/pemberton-residence/
- **Silver Ridge Residence** — Austin. The designer's own modernist home. https://www.jaycorder.com/projects/silver-ridge-residence/

# Interview question bank

Ask each architect a consistent set so you can compare. Group by theme; aim for 12–18 in a first meeting, save the rest for a second round.

**Process & collaboration style**
1. Walk us through your design process from first meeting to move-in. Who from your team is in the room at each phase, and who do we talk to day-to-day?
2. We want to co-design heavily — bringing our own sketches, references, and strong opinions. Describe a project where the client drove the aesthetic. How did that go, and where did you push back?
3. Are you more comfortable delivering your own design vision, or building on ours? Be honest — both are valid, we just need to know.
4. How do you handle disagreement when we want something you think is a mistake?

**MCM & aesthetic fit**
5. What does "mid-century modern" mean to you, and how is it different from contemporary modernism? Show us a project where you worked in that idiom.
6. Have you restored or designed a true MCM home (post-and-beam, clerestories, breeze blocks, flat/butterfly roofs, indoor-outdoor flow)? Which one, and what did you learn?
7. If we showed up with Eichler / Neutra / Fehr & Granger references, would that excite you or constrain you?

**Texas heat & site**
8. How do you design for Texas heat and sun — orientation, overhangs, shading, glazing, materials, passive cooling? Give a specific example.
9. We don't have a site yet. How involved do you get in site selection, and would you walk a candidate lot with us before we buy?
10. Tell us about a project where the site (slope, trees, views, floodplain, heritage oaks) fundamentally drove the design.

**Fees, money & change orders**
11. How do you structure fees — percentage of construction cost, fixed fee, or hourly with a cap? What percentage or number, and what's included/excluded?
12. How is the fee split across phases (programming, SD, DD, CDs, CA)?
13. How do you handle change orders and scope creep — especially with clients like us who'll want to revise as we go? What's billable vs. absorbed?
14. At what construction budget does a project like ours typically land per square foot in your hands, and what drives that number up or down?

**Communication & schedule**
15. What's your communication cadence — how often will we meet, and how do you share progress (models, renderings, walkthroughs)?
16. Realistically, when could you start, and what's your current queue? How many active projects does our lead architect carry at once?
17. What's a realistic design-through-move-in timeline for a $1–2M custom home, and where does it usually slip?

**Contractors, documents & construction**
18. Do you have builders you regularly work with? How do you run bidding and contractor selection?
19. How detailed are your construction documents, and how involved are you during construction (construction administration)? Do you do site visits?
20. How do you protect design intent during value-engineering when bids come in over budget?

**Sustainability & the hard questions**
21. What sustainability/energy strategies do you consider standard vs. upgrades for an Austin home?
22. Tell us about your last three *difficult* clients or projects — what went wrong and what you'd do differently.
23. What do you say no to? What kind of project or client is not a fit for you?
24. May we speak with two or three recent clients — ideally one whose project had a problem?
25. What's the one thing you wish clients understood before starting a custom home?

# How to actually engage

### Fee structures (Texas / Austin residential)
Three common models — get the actual structure in writing in the proposal:
- **Percentage of construction cost** (most common for full custom service). Broad cited range 5–20%; for Austin custom homes it clusters around **~9.5–14% for new construction** (one Austin firm, Webber + Studio, quotes 10–14% new / 12–18% remodel; another reference puts Austin residential at ~9.55–10.03%). Luxury/complex work trends to the upper end.
- **Fixed / flat fee** — works for well-defined simple scopes; risky for evolving co-design work (scope creep). Likely a poor fit for how you want to work.
- **Hourly with a not-to-exceed cap** — favored by some Austin architects for transparency on bespoke work; can suit heavy co-design if the cap is fair.
- Ignore "$/sf for design" quotes — practitioners consider them unreliable for custom work.

**Planning placeholder for a $1–2M build: budget roughly 10–15% of construction cost for full architectural services** (directional, not a quote). Sources: architecturebygeorge.com, ralstonarchitects.com.

### Engagement phases & rough fee split (AIA standard)
- **Programming** — ~5% (sometimes folded into SD). Define needs, room program, budget, site constraints.
- **Schematic Design (SD)** — ~15% (range 10–25%). Big-picture forms, plans, massing — *where heavy co-design lives*.
- **Design Development (DD)** — ~20% (range 10–25%). Materials, systems, details firmed up.
- **Construction Documents (CDs)** — ~40% (range 35–50%). The heaviest phase — permit/build drawings.
- **Bidding / Negotiation** — ~5%. Contractor selection, bid leveling.
- **Construction Administration (CA)** — ~20% (range 20–30%). Site visits, RFIs, protecting design intent through the build.

For a co-design couple, expect SD/DD to skew higher (more options, models, renderings) — discuss how that's billed. Sources: architecturalfees.com, aia.org.

### Timeline expectations (Austin custom home)
- **Total:** ~12–18 months for many custom homes; 18–24 months for larger / $3M-class projects. Your 12–24 month *move-in* target is feasible but tight if design + permitting + a complex site stack up — start architect selection now.
- **Design (pre-construction):** ~3–4 months of design + pre-con for higher-end work; heavy co-design and a true MCM scheme can push this longer.
- **Permitting:** ~60–120 days in most of Austin; can stretch toward a year with floodplain, **heritage-tree permits**, or impervious-cover review (very relevant to wooded Hill Country lots).
- **HOA architectural review:** add 30–60 days in communities like Spanish Oaks, Barton Creek, Rough Hollow.
- **Construction:** ~12–20 months — site/foundation (~1.5 mo), framing + rough systems (2–3 mo), exterior (1–2 mo), interior finishes (4+ mo).
Sources: newhousebuilder.com, jangelodesignbuild.com, trueluxfinehomes.com.

### What to bring to the first meeting
- **Budget:** construction budget + total all-in budget; ideally loan pre-approval done.
- **Site docs (if you have a lot):** boundary/topographic survey, plat, title, easements; geotechnical/soils report if available; photos; notes on views, slopes, access, existing structures, heritage trees, floodplain. (If you don't have a lot yet, say so — several of these firms will help evaluate candidate sites.)
- **Program / wish list:** room-by-room needs (architects often hand you a programming questionnaire); must-haves vs. nice-to-haves.
- **Inspiration:** your MCM reference images, plan sketches, material/finish ideas — essential for conveying the mid-century intent precisely.
- **Lifestyle notes:** what works/doesn't in your current home; how you entertain, work, and live indoor-outdoor.
Sources: rostarchitects.com, undercoverarchitect.com.

### Austin construction cost per square foot (2024–2025)
- **Full custom (Austin / Hill Country):** ~$325–$500/sf hard costs.
- **High-end architect-designed in premium areas** (Westlake, Zilker, central): $400–$600+/sf.
- **Ultra-luxury / premium communities** (e.g., Spanish Oaks): $500 to $800–$1,500/sf with rare materials/complexity.
- **Soft costs** (permits, design/engineering fees, insurance, financing): add ~10–25% of total, higher for architecturally significant projects.

**Budget math for $1–2M all-in:** a ~3,500 sf full-custom runs ~$1.1M–$1.75M in hard costs at $325–$500/sf; adding land, design, and site work pushes toward $1.4M–$2.2M total. So a $1–2M all-in budget realistically supports roughly **2,500–3,800 sf** of architect-designed MCM depending on finish level and lot — and **land cost is the biggest swing variable**. (Estimates, directional — not a bid.) Sources: levesqueco.com, neuhausre.com, rivendalehomestexas.com.
